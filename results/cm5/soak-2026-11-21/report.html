First hardware test of the square-end thin-bar fix (DroneBlocks/dexi_vision PR #2). The square-end 3 in 2-bar disc sat about 1.1 m from the camera all night, at 768x432 (640x360 and 15 fps in some runs).

Finding: an AprilTag in view can take over the disc search. At the first check, with the tag and the disc both in frame, the counter picked the AprilTag's black square as the disc (it is nearest the image center and passes min_circularity 0.70), read 0 bars and abstained, so nothing was published. With the tag masked out of a saved frame it read bars_2 at 0.91. Moving the sheet to the image center fixed it live. AprilTags and bridge cups can share a frame on the AVR field, so the disc search should reject square candidates (or skip a detected tag's quad).

Not tested: the rounded (avr-yolo) 2-bar disc. Only the square-end disc was on the sheet, and swapping discs needs someone at the bench.

YOLO note: YOLO read the square-end disc as bridge_3line (0.74) at the first check; it was trained on the rounded art. YOLO's bridge and blackout classes were left out of all scoring, per the handoff.

5. Video to the browser: rosbridge vs web_video_server

Stand-in client on a PC on the same network (tools/video_client.py), 10 min per mode, stack in the soak config. The rosbridge mode subscribes exactly like dexi-droneblocks components/CameraFeed.vue (CBOR, queue 1).

Result: a browser watching video costs about 3 points of board CPU and about 3 °C whichever path it uses. web_video_server saves only about 2% of a core over rosbridge with CBOR and delivers frames less evenly, so switching is not worth it on its own. Halving rosbridge's throttle_rate saves just 1.3% of a core, because rosbridge still receives all 30 fps and drops frames in Python: the same pattern as the YOLO and bar counter nodes. The lever is upstream: give video clients a stream at the rate they need instead of throttling inside each subscriber. Detection rates were unchanged in every mode. Earlier in the night a different browser client (minute 22 of the baseline) cost rosbridge 13% of a core; the Node-RED tag-nav dashboard, which sends frames as base64 JSON, is the likely heavier case.

6. Other findings

  • A rosbridge web client is expensive. In the baseline a browser connected for about 2 min (minute 22): board CPU 33% to 42% and +2.5 Â°C, rosbridge +13% of a core, micro_ros_agent +4%. See section 5 for the video path.
  • Re-selecting a CPU governor resets its tunables. Pi OS boots ondemand with up_threshold 50, sampling_rate 100000, sampling_down_factor 50. Switching away and back gives the kernel defaults (95 / 10000 / 1), and the board then runs at about 2200 MHz instead of 2390 and about 4.5 Â°C cooler (tested cleanly as ondemand_kernel_defaults).
  • NV12 crashes camera_ros on this camera (std::runtime_error on the stream format; 0 fps). YUYV works but costs more than XRGB8888.
  • /cam0/image_raw/compressed_2hz has no subscriber in any DroneBlocks repo or in the board's Node-RED flows. Removing its throttle saved 1.9 pts.
  • Node-RED tag-nav dashboard also streams video through rosbridge (5 fps, plain JSON, so frames are base64), and the pi_camera_subscriber example uses /image_raw/compressed, a topic this image does not publish.
  • The installed cm5 launch file is older than its source: the source has an image_throttle_raw_node (2 Hz raw images) that the install lacks. A rebuild would add a node that copies raw frames.
  • Seeded config shadowing (dexi-os #54): ~/.dexi-config.yaml had tag_nav on with tag_nav_dexi5.yaml on a DEXI-3. Turned off for the soak.
  • Thermal state at start: 79 Â°C at 50 min uptime with get_throttled 0x80000 (soft limit already hit once since boot), on a bench supply with a desk fan.
  • Building C++ on the 2 GB board with the stack running runs it out of memory (this test: swap full, SSH unresponsive for about 40 min, the kernel killed the compiler). Stop dexi.service and build with -j1.
  • Profiler overhead: the soak profiler itself used about 11.6% of one core (shown as python3) in every run, so it cancels out in comparisons.

8. Method

Profiler: dexi_bench/profilers/soak.py on the board, 1 Hz: CPU busy % (100 = all 4 cores), vcgencmd temperature, ARM clock and throttle flags, MemAvailable, swap, per-process CPU (100 = one core), and per-pipeline message counts and target hits from ROS subscriptions. It never subscribes to an image topic; camera rate comes from /cam0/camera_info. Sweep: dexi_bench/profilers/soak_sweep.py: restore pristine config, apply one change, restart dexi.service, 3 min warm-up, 15 min measurement, restore. Each change is compared with the baseline runs on either side of it, in the same CPU state. Raw data: runs/<name>/ (system.csv, procs.csv.gz, detections.csv, summary.json, change.diff, dexi.journal).

What went wrong during the soak, and what was done: (1) the first no_throttle_2hz edit added a duplicate condition= and the stack did not start; fixed and re-run. (2) The governor tests left ondemand with kernel-default tunables, so five later runs (Fast DDS UDP, Cyclone, YUYV, NV12, baseline@4) ran at about 2200 MHz; they are compared only with baseline@4, and the board was rebooted to restore the original state. (3) Phase 4 first failed to start (set -u against ROS setup.bash), and its photo and disk-guard steps were redone separately. (4) The web_video_server build ran the board out of memory; rebuilt with the stack stopped.
